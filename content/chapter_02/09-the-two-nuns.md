## §31–32

As known, **in *Binah* there is a bent *Nun* (נ) and a straight *Nun* (ן).** [▶ 4:34](listen:020#274) Within *bina* itself there are a bent *nun* and a straight *nun*, and the same within *tevuna*. Taken generally, between the two, *bina* is the bent *nun* and *tevuna* the straight one. [▶ 4:38](listen:020#278) That is, **the bent *Nun* (נ) is the width, while the straight *Nun* (ן) is the length.** [▶ 4:52](listen:020#292)

Furthermore, **when expanded, the *Nun* which fills it is the straight *Nun***: the *milui* (spelling out) of the letter is נו״ן, a bent *nun*, a *vav*, and a straight *nun*, **which is not the case with the expansion of any other letters.** [▶ 5:03](listen:020#303)

One might ask: the letter *mem* also ends with a *mem*, and *vav* with a *vav*. With *mem*, the open and the closed *mem* are two levels of the same thing. What is special in *nun* is that its *milui* is a *nun*, a *vav* (“and”) and a *nun*. The *vav* both separates them and unites them: “and” means there are two things, and that they are connected. So in *bina* there are two separate matters, not just two levels: *bina* and *tevuna*. [▶ 6:04](listen:020#364)

## §33

**This is because the length is included in the width**: the expansion of the length is included in the width. If one has the width of the whole explanation in his head, he can bring it down and explain it to others; without the width, he cannot. [▶ 7:27](listen:020#447) **This is likewise the case with *Tvunah***: it too has a length and a width. [▶ 7:55](listen:020#475)

As for **the double *Nun* in the matter which is called *Hitbonenut***, **this is the inner aspect of *Binah*, which is called *Iyun*, to stand upon the subject**, over and over again. [▶ 8:02](listen:020#482)

## §34

**This is called *Binah* of *Binah* and is an intermediary between *Binah* and *Tvunah*, through the aspect of the straight *Nun*.** [▶ 8:22](listen:020#502) As said before, it is specifically *bina sheb'bina* that brings about the *kelita*. How does one get from *bina* to *tevuna*? Through *hisbonenus*, the intermediary between them. One has grasped the idea with *bina*; how does he come to the *tevuna* of it, to apply it to outside things and teach it to others? Through *bina sheb'bina*, because *bina sheb'bina* brings about *kelita*. There is no separate power of *kelita*. [▶ 8:29](listen:020#509)

So *bina sheb'bina* is the main power of all the powers, the linchpin; and it is the one in one's own hands. The other powers are not really in one's hands. Even *daas*, which is partly in one's hands (one can force himself to concentrate), really comes from *bina sheb'bina*. [▶ 9:18](listen:020#558)

It works **through the aspect of the straight *Nun***, the length of *bina*: *hisbonenus* within *bina* is the straight *nun*. [▶ 13:15](listen:020#795) *Chachma* of *bina*, coming up with new things, is the width. *Kelita* comes not from new things but from going over the same explanation once more, along the same length. “Fine, we understood it, now let us learn another *Bava*” is width, and it will not bring *kelita*. As any college student knows, to absorb the material one has to read it again. [▶ 13:36](listen:020#816)

So far, then: the first chapter explained how *bina sheb'bina* activates all the powers above it, and explained *daas* separately without saying how it is activated. Now he has explained that *daas*, *ben u'vas*, is activated specifically from *tevuna*, and *tevuna* from *bina sheb'bina*, going over the length one more time. [▶ 14:30](listen:020#870)

> **Key point:** This also explains the Tanya's difference between one who serves G-d and one who does not, between learning a matter a hundred times and a hundred and one times. The hundred times were to understand it; that makes a *maskil*. The hundred and first, once it is already understood, is where *kelita* begins: it brings *daas* into the matter and moves the *middos*, and that makes an *oved*. [▶ 15:08](listen:020#908)
