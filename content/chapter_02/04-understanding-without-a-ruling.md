## §9–10

**An example of all this can be seen in the depth of the logical debate of the Talmud, in which there are many reasons and explanations to argue one way or another**. [▶ 19:44](listen:018#1184) Some people can master a whole *pilpul* (Talmudic debate) and understand all its *sevaros* (lines of reasoning), yet cannot apply it to anything: **he still is incapable of bringing to light any actual verdict or ruling of “innocent” or “guilty” from the essential intellect.** [▶ 19:52](listen:018#1192)

In a way the power of *tevuna* has a higher root than the power of *bina*, because to bring a matter out one must understand it more deeply than the original understanding. [▶ 20:09](listen:018#1209) The Greek philosophers were in one way greater than the modern scientists, yet because the modern scientists applied the ideas, they came to understand the principles better than the Greeks did with their *bina*. [▶ 20:27](listen:018#1227) Even among the Greeks, the famous story of the clever Greek is about *tevuna*, not *bina*: Archimedes sat and thought and never tried to apply anything, until the king had a problem that needed an application. Because he had to apply it, he understood the thing more deeply, and so came the “eureka” and the principle of the displacement of water. [▶ 21:25](listen:018#1285)

So someone may understand the Talmud, while the great *poskim* (halachic authorities), like the Rambam, could apply it to an actual ruling of guilty or innocent. [▶ 22:26](listen:018#1346)

## §11–12

In other words, **one is not capable of bringing out any “guilty” verdict from this reasoning, nor any “innocent” verdict from this reasoning.** [▶ 22:40](listen:018#1360) One can learn the whole subject in the Talmud, but actually to rule on it he will not be able to do. [▶ 22:50](listen:018#1370)

**This is because, as of yet, these concepts and explanations are only theoretical abstractions.** [▶ 23:02](listen:018#1382) To find guilty or innocent through them, to **state a “guilty” or an “innocent” verdict**, is the matter of *chesed* and *gevura* (kindness and severity), and *chesed* and *gevura* do not come out of *bina*; they come specifically from *tevuna*. [▶ 23:11](listen:018#1391)

## §13

Rather, **it is like a *Heyulie* power, in which it is not yet known what will result from it or what could be done with it.** [▶ 23:28](listen:018#1408) It is just an idea, and nobody knows what will come out of it or what can be done with it. [▶ 23:38](listen:018#1418) The Yiddish line in the Hebrew text says it plainly: the concept by itself, as it is, one can do nothing with it, to lean (*noteh*) either to merit or to guilt. [▶ 23:45](listen:018#1425)

## §14

It follows that **the power of *Tvunah*, which spreads forth from the mind of *Binah*, is the ability to bring the essential comprehension, to an aspect of spreading down from it, in all matters that are separate** from the concept itself: to a verdict of guilt or innocence, or to **the arousal [of the emotions] of the heart**, for he knows that this matter will bring *hispaalus* (emotional arousal). [▶ 24:09](listen:018#1449) The words **bringing it into thought in many various angles** open what the book discusses later: the letters of thought come from *Malchus* of *tevuna*. [▶ 24:29](listen:018#1469)

From the recap at the start of the next class: *tevuna* is the power of *bina* to spread out, to bring the essence of the comprehension downward, into separate things and into the *middos* (emotions). [▶ 0:33](listen:019#33) But *tevuna* can also go up, as *bina* can; it is a full *bina* by itself. The power of *chachma sheb'bina* (discovering a new thing within the same thing, off to the side) originates in *tevuna*, and so does *bina sheb'chachma*, understanding one thing from another. That is why one who has *bina sheb'chachma* is called a *navon*, a man of *tevuna*, and not a *mevin*. [▶ 1:06](listen:019#66) *Chachma sheb'bina* comes specifically from *kelita*, not from *bina*, which only understands the concept itself. This is why the opening definition says only that *tevuna* is the *spreading* of the light of *bina*, not the spreading down: spreading means in every direction. [▶ 3:04](listen:019#184)
