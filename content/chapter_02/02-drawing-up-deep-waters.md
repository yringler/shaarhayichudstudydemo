## §4

**This is analogous to one who draws out deep waters to quench the thirst of the sheep.** [▶ 3:08](listen:018#188)

> **Mashal:** There are two ways to give the sheep water from a deep well. One can throw each sheep into the well and pull it out again, or one can take the water out and bring it up to the sheep. [▶ 3:22](listen:018#202)

First there has to be a man of *bina*. He goes deep into the well itself; he does not bring the water out. He can drink because he is all the way down at the level of the water, in the depths, but that does not help the sheep. The man of *tevuna* takes that water and brings it from deep water to shallow water. [▶ 3:34](listen:018#214)

So **when the reasoning of a concept is too deep to be retained in the comprehension of man, this is called “deep waters,”** and again this book is itself the example: it speaks of very deep matters, the deep waters of the *taalumos chachma* (hidden depths of wisdom). A well is dug down to the aquifer, and the water stays down in the aquifer. That is **the depth of the comprehension (*Omek HaMoosag*), from which the length and width come**. [▶ 4:09](listen:018#249)

## §5

**It is specifically one who possesses the power of *Tvunah* who can “draw it out.”** [▶ 4:53](listen:018#293) Here the Mitteler Rebbe draws it up, like the pail drawing water from a well. In other words, **he brings to light the aspect of the hidden depth of the concept, until it may be retained within the comprehension of man, as if it was not deep at all**, so that a regular person without such a deep mind, like us, can hold it. [▶ 5:16](listen:018#316)

Even earlier generations with very great minds could not go into this depth, and here we are receiving it. Why? Because the man of *tevuna* brought it out to our level, the way water is brought from the aquifer to the sheep. [▶ 6:07](listen:018#367) It is deep by any standard, yet a regular college student, with a little explanation, could learn it. [▶ 6:33](listen:018#393) Like one who draws water from the well, **he brings the depth [itself] close** to the sheep. [▶ 6:45](listen:018#405)

## §6

One must say that **the power of *Tvunah* is a power that spreads forth from the power of *Binah***. [▶ 7:02](listen:018#422) It is not the power of *bina* itself. The power of *bina* does not draw the water out of the aquifer; it goes down into the aquifer, like a man with a drill who must himself go down into the depth in order to drink. [▶ 7:08](listen:018#428) The power of *tevuna* spreads out from the power of *bina*: without the understanding into the thing, one cannot bring it out. It depends on having the power of *bina* first, **to bring its depth into revelation below to the recipients**. [▶ 7:38](listen:018#458)

Of the two, *bina* and *tevuna*, *tevuna* is the *orech* (length). [▶ 8:03](listen:018#483) The length of *bina* is the power to explain the matter to oneself; the length of *tevuna* is the power to explain it to somebody else. Each of them has its own width and length, but as a pair *tevuna* is the length and *bina* is the *rochav* (width): *bina* spreads out into all the different parts. Compared to *chachma*, *chachma* is called the width and *bina* the length; within *bina*, *bina* is the width and *tevuna* the length. [▶ 9:01](listen:018#541)
