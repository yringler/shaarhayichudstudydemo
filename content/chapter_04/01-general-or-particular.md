## §1

This chapter is about the particular: it teaches that the material has to be learned in a particular manner. [▶ 0:07](listen:022#7) **We must now explain how one should contemplate**: in a general way (*derech klal*) or specifically in a way of particulars (*derech prat*)? The text gives an example from the contemplation of how G-dliness fills the three worlds of *Beriya*, *Yetzira* and *Asiya* (*memalei kol almin*, the light that fills all worlds). In the general comprehension of this, **all the examples of the ray [emanating from the luminary], or of the spreading forth [of Divine influence], or of the letters [of speech] etc., are all rooted in one general matter**, the revelation of what was hidden.

## §2

Even the general way has **many particular ways to [understand] this**: there are many analogies (*meshalim*) even in a *derech klal*. [▶ 0:43](listen:022#43) In general, the *Aibershter* fills the world the way the soul fills the body, or like the sun, and so on. And He is totally separate from it, as an unlimited Being is from a created, limited one. Practically everybody is aware of these ideas in a general way, if not with all the analogies.

So a *derech klal* does not mean a single way with no length, breadth and depth (*orech*, *rochav* and *omek*). It has many ways, and even within it there are many particular matters, as the text will explain later. **[nonetheless], it all is [understood in a] general [manner]**.

## §3

The alternative is to contemplate **in a manner of particulars, [analyzing] each world, each creature and each of the various particular levels in the [entire] chaining down [of the worlds], from cause to [subsequent] cause**. [▶ 1:46](listen:022#106) This is more than knowing that the *Aibershter* fills the world like a soul fills a body. Even of the soul and body, everybody knows in general that the soul fills the body. But then there is the question of how it works in the eye, and how it makes a tooth grow: each detail in a particular way, each one by itself.

## §4–7

The same question arises about **the contemplation of the “Upper Unity,”** (*yichuda ila'a*): **the lights and vessels [of the world] of *Atzilut*, including all the details of the ten *sefirot*** up to the essence of the *Or Ein Sof*. [▶ 2:28](listen:022#148) Should one contemplate it in general: how the light of *Atzilus* comes from G-d's essence through contraction (*tzimtzum*), and how it is incomparable to the Emanator? [▶ 2:37](listen:022#157)

Or how, in general, **“He and His life force are one”**: how the *sefiros* of *Atzilus* are truly unified, and how the infinite light is contracted in order to come into them. [▶ 2:46](listen:022#166) This too has a great many details, as will be explained. It can be understood generally **through various analogies**, such as the flame bound to the coal mentioned in *Sefer Yetzira*, or the way the soul's faculties are revealed from their concealment in the soul's essence. [▶ 3:08](listen:022#188)

## §8

Through this one comes to understand the depth of it: that the worlds of *Beriya*, *Yetzira* and *Asiya* are full of G-dliness, understood in a general way, or that the world of *Atzilus* is unified with the Emanator. [▶ 3:20](listen:022#200) So the subjects of *yichuda ila'a* and *yichuda tata'a* (the higher and the lower unity) can both be discussed in a general manner.

But what the text calls "a general manner" is in fact a particular manner compared to what most people take this contemplation to be. [▶ 3:52](listen:022#232) Most people, especially in our days, do not go even this far. They say: G-d fills everything, it is all one, the world is the universe and the universe is the world. And they figure they have already grasped it.
