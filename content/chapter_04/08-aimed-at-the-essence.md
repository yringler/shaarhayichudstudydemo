## §40

As a rule, **a general thought and a particular thought cannot exist simultaneously**: one cannot think about two things at once. If you are thinking before Whom you stand, you are thinking of that; if you are thinking of *Malchus* of *tevuna*, you are thinking of that. [▶ 11:37](listen:023#697)

Nevertheless, **he who delves with all his heart and with all his desire, specifically to [contemplate] the essence** of G-dliness directs even his study of particulars to the general thought of the Essence. [▶ 12:03](listen:023#723) It may seem that we learn one particular, then another; now only about *bina*, now about *chachma*, as though it were human psychology. But since the depth and desire are directed to the essence of G-dliness, we know that this is not at all what it is about: the intention (*kavana*) is truly to the Essence. [▶ 12:36](listen:023#756) Not only that: his thought is more on the Essence than that of someone who is focused on the Essence itself. [▶ 12:49](listen:023#769)

## §41

**He will not fall from the general intention at all, as a result of focusing on the particulars.** [▶ 12:55](listen:023#775) The intention to the essence of the *Aibershter* is not disturbed by talking about small details, even when, as said before, the subject seems to be not *tevuna*, *bina*, *chachma* or *Malchus* of *Atzilus*, but our own particular *kelipa*. [▶ 13:04](listen:023#784) Since he is truly directed to the *Aibershter*, it does not disturb. On the contrary, it is what makes the intention proper.

This is **because all the particulars are drawn to and follow the general principle**: all the particulars he thinks about follow only the general, the *Aibershter* Himself. [▶ 13:43](listen:023#823) As known to whoever learns in the proper way, directed only to the *Aibershter*, and as the Sages said, **“[pray] to Him rather than to His qualities.”** The whole intention of this study is to the *Aibershter* Himself, and has nothing to do with the attributes (*middos*) as such. [▶ 14:10](listen:023#850)

## §42–43

In other words, **the main intent of the matter of contemplating the particulars is solely for the specific purpose of coming to the Essential Self and being** of G-d Himself. That is the main thing one intends. [▶ 14:31](listen:023#871)

And **one should never consider the particulars to be the main thing, such as studying a subject for itself**, as someone does who wants to learn a thing for himself. [▶ 14:40](listen:023#880) An etymologist or a psychologist wants to learn what *bina* is and what *chachma* is, and that is what he is really learning. What is learned here has nothing to do with *bina* and *chachma* as such: we are learning about the *Aibershter*. [▶ 15:01](listen:023#901)

## §44–45

**Likewise, one’s analysis of the Zohar and the writings of the Ari’zal must specifically be in this manner.** Some people want to look in the *Zohar* to find out about demons or the like; but all of it must be specifically in this way. **This will suffice those of understanding.** [▶ 15:24](listen:023#924)

The author, the *Mitteler Rebbe*, adds that his father, the *Alter Rebbe*, received this teaching **from the mouth of the *Rav,* the *Maggid*** of Mezritch, **with a full explanation**, and that he heard it from his father's mouth. [▶ 15:50](listen:023#950)
