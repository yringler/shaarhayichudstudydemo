## §37

Consider **one who has not delved his concentration into particular unifications, but, [rather], begins with the general aspect** says that everything comes from the infinite light. [▶ 8:30](listen:023#510) Even if he works hard at it and does not stop at the bare statement, but thinks about the soul in the body and the flame from the coal, and thinks hard about exactly how this nullification is, still **the truth of the matter will not become as embedded in his soul**. It will not be fixed in his soul and mind as it is after real, thorough analysis (*iyun*) of every particular, from which he comes to the general.

## §38

This is the meaning of “**the particular requires the general and the general requires the particular**”: to do it properly, one cannot get stuck on the particulars either. After the particulars, one has to unify them into a general whole. [▶ 9:26](listen:023#566) Someone who knows only the screws, and not how they go together, will never build an airplane.

> **Mashal:** The blind men and the elephant: one thought an elephant is like a palm tree, another that it is like a broom, because none of them realized that what he held was only a particular. [▶ 9:53](listen:023#593)

So the particular way by itself is also not good. That is the way of the scientists, who learn the particulars and never try to put them together into a whole, and so they think the particulars somehow work by themselves. [▶ 10:17](listen:023#617) For **no general [concept] can exist without particulars**, and no particular without the general: to understand a thing properly one must know all its particulars, and knowing them all, one can understand the whole. [▶ 10:39](listen:023#639)

## §39

**This matter has already been debated by many of the foremost *Rishonim,* such as the Rambam and the Tur.** [▶ 10:51](listen:023#651) It concerns the service of prayer, which is the whole point of contemplating and going up. What is one supposed to think? When saying "Yours, G-d, is the greatness," should one think of greatness, and at "the might" think of might, and so on through all the intentions (*kavanos*)? **Or, whether one’s intent should rather be to realize, in a general way, before Whom he is standing and praying?** That is: simply to think, "I am standing before the *Aibershter*." [▶ 11:09](listen:023#669)
