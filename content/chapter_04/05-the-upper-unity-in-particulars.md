## §22–23

**Likewise, the contemplation of the “Upper Unity” should be in a manner of particulars**: in all the details of the ten *sefiros* and the *partzufim* (statures) of *Zeir Anpin* and *Nukva*, *Abba* and *Ima*, *Arich Anpin* and *Atik Yomin*, knowing how they work and how each connects with the next. [▶ 27:39](listen:022#1659) It is not enough to say that *Zeir Anpin* is G-d's light. One should know exactly how *Zeir Anpin* and *Nukva* come from *Abba* and *Ima*, and how *Abba* and *Ima* come from *Arich* and *Atik*.

**It is specifically [the contemplation of all this], in a manner of particulars**, that leads **the general light of the essence of the Emanator, which includes and unifies everything**, into the soul. That is what one really wants to know, not even *Malchus* of *Atzilus*. [▶ 28:17](listen:022#1697) From *Malchus* of *Atzilus* one goes to the union of *Zeir Anpin* and *Nukva*, from there to the union of *Abba* and *Ima*, then to how it comes down from *Arich* and *Atik*, and how they come out from *Adam Kadmon*, up to the beginning of the line (*Kav*). Then how the *Kav* comes out after the impression left by the contraction, and before that, the contraction and the infinite light. [▶ 28:30](listen:022#1710)

Otherwise it is only: "There is an infinite light." And what is the infinite light? Like a light bulb, a big light bulb: that is basically how people understand it. Why all these *partzufim*? "There's a G-d, and finished." No: it is specifically knowing the particulars that leads to the general essence of the Emanator. [▶ 28:57](listen:022#1737)

## §24–25

However, **this is not the case if one only contemplates in a general manner**, thinking only that there is a G-d in the world: then he sees G-d from very far away. [▶ 29:32](listen:022#1772) Then **it is possible for him to fall into false delusions, as do fools and those who lack** understanding, even into total idolatry. [▶ 29:46](listen:022#1786)

This comes **because they focus their entire mind solely on one general matter**: he concentrates his whole mind on just one thing, and that is a general, vague matter. [▶ 0:38](listen:023#38) Such people sit and meditate on the depth of everything, that there is a G-d and He is infinite, and they actually have visions and false imaginations. [▶ 0:08](listen:023#8)

## §26–27

**However, the focusing and delving of one’s concentration into each detail is the opposite of error and distance**. [▶ 0:55](listen:023#55) Chapter 1 said that one must have depth of *daas* (*amkus hadaas*), the *kli* (vessel) for everything. That is true, but the *kli* is not to sit and meditate on the final general idea one is meant to reach. It is to contemplate every particular and have *amkus hadaas* in every particular: while learning this particular, one is wholly focused on understanding this particular and no other. [▶ 1:08](listen:023#68) That is the opposite of error, and of seeing the thing only in a far-off, vague way that can be turned upside down.

**On the contrary, through this, the matter becomes closer**, as **he goes from one particular to a higher particular until he unifies them all in a general unification**. [▶ 1:48](listen:023#108) It is no longer seen from afar: he has connected all the particulars and knows the thing with all its details, as a doctor knows the body, or as the engineer knows the airplane. [▶ 2:09](listen:023#129)
