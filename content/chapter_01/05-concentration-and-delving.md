## §21

**We find that this aspect of *Iyun* is only an aspect of a receptacle for the delving of *Binah* itself.** This sums up what has been said so far: *iyun* (close analysis), which is *bina sheb'bina*, is a *kli* (vessel) for *hasaga* (comprehension), the depth of the concept (*omek hamusag*), which is the *Keser* (crown) of *bina*. The learning is the vessel for understanding the explanation. Though the text does not say it here, the chain goes on: *hasaga*, understanding the explanation, is a *kli* for *havana*, the outer level of *chachma*, where one sees the thing itself and not only its explanation. That is a *kli* for *taalumos chachma*, seeing the inner essence of the thing, which is already in *Keser*, the higher knowing (*daas elyon*); and that in turn is a vessel for a level higher still, where one experiences the thing itself with nothing of oneself involved. Each lower level is a *kli* for a higher one. [▶ 0:35](listen:015#35)

Now the text turns downward. **at first glance, this aspect of *Iyun* is called “the delving of the concentration” in common usage**: everyone calls it contemplation or meditation, the way *Kuntres HaHisbonenus* is called the tract on meditation. **[In truth], this is not the case**, for **the delving of the concentration is only a vessel**. At every level concentration is the outer vessel. Without it *bina sheb'bina* will not work; going from the depth of the concept to the actual grasp of the thing also needs *daas*, and so does going from the outer *havana* of *chachma* to its inner level. Once one knows the explanation, one has to concentrate on it to go deeper. So *daas* is an instrument for going deeper, not the depth itself; in fact it is an instrument for an instrument, since *bina sheb'bina*, which works on the explanation, is what actually gets one there. [▶ 2:45](listen:015#165)

> **Key point:** This is the whole mistake of idol worship: treating *daas* as the primary instrument. One concentrates, thinking "G-d, G-d, G-d," and expects the concentration to bring one there. But *daas* is a vessel for going deeper into a matter one already has, not for reaching it. *Daas* needs content, and it does not supply the content; *bina sheb'bina* does. [▶ 5:02](listen:015#302)

## §22

**is what is called “delving” (*Ma’amik*)**. *Ma'amik* is also concentration. Concentration on *bina sheb'bina* is the lower kind, *ha'amakas hadaas*. *Ma'amik* is concentrating on the depth of the concept, even when one holds it by way of a *mashal* (analogy), thinking it over and over, and this reaches **higher than even *Chochmah* itself**. The outer level of *chachma* is seeing the explanation, and not only the explanation but what it is about; yet it is not seeing the actual thing. That is *taalumos chachma*, which reaches the root of the thing, so that through it one can see what other things are about as well. [▶ 6:16](listen:015#376)

> **Mashal:** In physics there is the explanation. Then there is the real physicist, who already understands more than the explanation. But that does not mean he has the root of physics, which is like metaphysics, and the root of physics would answer questions in other fields too. That is *taalumos chachma*. [▶ 7:59](listen:015#479)

**Now, seemingly, *Chochmah* is literally an aspect of the intangible “nothingness” of the concept**: *chachma* is the concept before it takes any particular form or explanation, whereas the depth of the concept is the depth of this particular explanation. [▶ 8:41](listen:015#521)

## §23

**its source reaches into the very source of the flow of the waters of the spring of *Chochmah***. A scientist can look deeply into physics and gain all kinds of insights, yet never look deeper still: how did the whole thing come into existence in the first place? Even their own language shows what is missing. They speak of the laws of nature, and a law means that someone makes it; where does the law come from? What is the root of the *muskal* (the concept)? If one does not keep thinking about the root, one may reach the *ayin* (nothing) where *chachma* is grasped not as an explanation, and go no further. The root is where the whole spring of *chachma* comes from, **which is called “the depths of *Chochmah*”**, its hidden parts. And just as *bina*, called *yesh* (something), has depth, length and width, so does the spring of *chachma*, called *ayin*. [▶ 9:23](listen:015#563)

## §24

**The depth of the spring is the beginning and source of its inception, at its very depth.** Under the ground is the aquifer, a giant ocean beneath the ground from which the spring comes.

> **Mashal:** One sees one spring and another spring, but both connect to the same *taalumos chachma*. On the surface they are two different things: this one is in Pittsburgh, that one in California. Go down far enough, and there is one big ocean both come from. So too, follow physics or psychology to its ultimate conclusion, and they come from the same source. [▶ 11:11](listen:015#671)

**the wellsprings of the abyss burst forth**: what is called the spring is really only its tail, its end, like the *Malchus* (kingship) of the spring and not its *Keser*. **the earth is riddled with fissures** and veins, as with oil. What one sees above is not the real essence of the spring; what makes the spring is its **primary depth**, the aquifer. [▶ 12:19](listen:015#739)

About this it is said, **“*Chochmah* is [found] from nothing,”** that is, from the hidden depth of *chachma*. Compared with that depth, *chachma* itself is already something that exists (*metzius*); the real *ayin* of *chachma* is *taalumos chachma*. [▶ 13:51](listen:015#831)
