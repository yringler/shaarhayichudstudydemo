## §25

**Now, *Chochmah* is the discovery of new insight which emerges like a bolt of lightning**. But the lightning is already the *yesh* of *chachma*: even in revealed *chachma*, the flash is where the idea can be brought out, its connection to the outside. **The source of its inception in its hidden depths is its innermost essence**: the real *chachma* is *taalumos chachma* in *Keser*. That is why "*chachma*" in Kabbalistic and Chassidic books often means the *chachma* of *Arich*: when it is said that Torah comes from *chachma*, it means the *chachma* of *Arich*, not the revealed *chachma* of *Atzilus*. [▶ 14:03](listen:015#843)

Here *bina* serves as a *mashal* for *chachma*. The depth of the concept is **the depth of the comprehension of *Binah* which is felt in a tangible way, with revealed explanations**: a *sevara* (line of reasoning) is already a *yesh*, like a theory. **The depth of this explanation of the concept is only the aspect of the “nothing of the something” of comprehension**, the *ayin* of the *yesh* of this explanation, and it **is of no comparison to the “nothing” of *Chochmah* before it comes into the category of comprehension altogether**. So there is a ladder: the depth of the concept is the *ayin* of the *yesh*; the flash is the *yesh* of the *ayin*; deeper is the *ayin* of the *ayin*; and deepest of all is the *ayin* in *taalumos chachma*, which has nothing to do with this particular wisdom. All wisdoms come from it, not just this spring but all the springs. [▶ 16:21](listen:015#981)

The text calls this depth **its essential point**; the word is *tamtzis*. A *tamtzis* is an essence in the sense of an extract: what remains after something has been processed and its taste drawn out, as instant coffee is the *tamtzis* of coffee beans. [▶ 18:19](listen:015#1099)

> **Mashal:** Children learn addition with "one fishy plus one fishy is two fishies," then with many examples and homework. Eventually one knows how to add any numbers. Not because the brain holds every sum, like 2,500 plus 7,600, but because it holds the *tamtzis* of the whole thing: the examples are thrown away and the way to add remains. That *tamtzis* is the depth of the concept. [▶ 19:48](listen:015#1188)

It is explained elsewhere (he thinks in *Kuntres HaHispaalus*) that there is a summary before contemplation and a summary after it. A story is told of the Deitschel (some say the Alter Rebbe with his father-in-law): asked what he had learned, he said, "That there is a G-d in the world." Told that even the maid knows that, he answered, "She thinks there is a G-d in the world. I know there is a G-d in the world." On the surface the two summaries look exactly the same; the difference is that he had the *tamtzis* that comes afterward, and, as the text will explain, there is more even after the *tamtzis*. [▶ 21:29](listen:015#1289)

## §26

**the depth of the comprehension, which is called the 50th gate of *Binah*** raises a question. The *shaar hanun* (fiftieth gate) of *bina* is not so simple: even *Moshe Rabbeinu* had a hard time reaching it. How can it be just the *tamtzis* of the explanation? There is depth, and there is the depth of the depth. The fiftieth gate is in *bina*, bringing the *ayin* of *chachma* into the *yesh* of an explanation, and it starts narrow: **its beginning will be like the antennae of a grasshopper**. That is also why *bina* is called a shofar, narrow at one end and wide at the other, like the horn of plenty. [▶ 23:21](listen:015#1401)

Still, **its source also reaches into the depths of *Chochmah* itself**. The first depth is the *Keser* of *bina* of *Atzilus*. But just as *chachma* has a depth in the *chachma* of *Arich*, there is also *bina* of *Arich*, and the fiftieth gate keeps going higher, to *bina* of *Atik* and beyond. [▶ 25:06](listen:015#1506)

**It is clearly observable** in this very study. The book delves into explanations: the first chapters explain what *hisbonenus* is, and from chapter 10 on it explains how the world came into existence, *seder hishtalshelus* (the chain of descending worlds). One delves only into the explanation, yet it brings a **new light of *Chochmah* (insight) into *Binah***. As one of the participants in these classes often says, each time he reads the book it is a whole new book. It is the same book with exactly the same letters, but now he sees it in a new light of *chachma* in *bina*. Even things that are not explained in the book at all come from the book. [▶ 26:34](listen:015#1594)

**This, then, is what is called *Ma’amik* – delving, which is an active verb**. *Ha'amakas hadaas* is concentrating while one first learns, as in the class itself. *Ma'amik* comes afterward, walking around thinking it over: what does it really mean, what is its root? It activates the depth of *chachma* and *bina*, in *Arich*, in *Atik*, and beyond. [▶ 28:20](listen:015#1700)

> **Key point:** In this *bina* has an advantage over *chachma* (he is not sure whether the text says it later). When the fiftieth gate reaches into the depth of *chachma*, it grasps it as a *yesh*, so it can be brought out into an explanation and to other people. Someone who has only a spiritual insight or intuitive feeling may be unable to explain it, and may even lose it himself a few days later, like inventors (he mentions the story of Edison) who forgot a flash of insight unless they wrote it down at once. The fiftieth gate of *bina* goes as deep as the depth of *chachma*, but holds it as a *yesh*. [▶ 29:21](listen:015#1761)
