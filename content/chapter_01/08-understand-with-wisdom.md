## §29

**This is the power of explanation within *Chochmah* itself**: *bina* of *chachma*, the inherent, *hiyuli* (raw, potential) power of explanation within an insight. Once one understands something beyond its explanation, as the *ayin* of *chachma*, it is inherent in that *chachma* that it can be explained. One has not explained it yet, but if someone asks, one will be able to bring it **into many different explanations and facets**, not just one. Yet **it is still higher than the comprehension of *Binah***: he has not actually explained it; it is the potential for explanation. [▶ 43:13](listen:015#2593)

## §30–31

**He could not find ‘men of understanding’**. *Bina sheb'chachma* has two aspects. Going downward, it is the power of explanation just described, which brings *chachma* into the many explanations of *bina*. But when the sages said there were no men of understanding, they did not mean there was no one who could explain. [▶ 44:22](listen:015#2662)

The real, inner *bina* of *chachma* is **one who understands one thing from another**: understanding a deeper wisdom from an outer one. And because this power **reaches into the depths of *Chochmah***, into the aquifer that is the source of all wisdoms, **he will be capable of understanding a completely different wisdom**, a different field altogether. So this power can go down, it can go up, and it can go sideways. Sideways is like a width: it brings the insight into another wisdom that may be on the same level as this one. [▶ 0:05](listen:016#5)

> **Mashal:** The Wright brothers (not truly men of understanding, he notes, but good as an outer *mashal*) were bicycle makers. They did not look at a motor as just a car motor; they looked into its root, a supplier of power, and saw that as it powers a car it could power an airplane. Someone at a more external level would see a bicycle chain only as part of a bicycle and never think how it applies to anything else. They saw it as a way to move energy from the feet to the wheel through the chain and gears, from one thing to another, and the first airplanes drove the propeller that way. [▶ 2:10](listen:016#130)

## §32

**This [aspect of understanding] is much greater in level than that of a *Chacham*.** The outer, downward side, explaining outward, is lower than *chachma sheb'chachma*. But the inner *bina sheb'chachma*, the *navon*, is higher than the wise man, who is *chachma sheb'chachma*. The wise man has an invention; the *navon* may not come up with a new invention, but he can take an old invention and bring out something wholly new. In a way it has a higher source, reaching deeper than *chachma* itself, into understanding one thing from another. [▶ 4:14](listen:016#254)

Remember that the subject is what *hisbonenus* means in *Chassidus*. *Bina sheb'bina* activates *chachma sheb'bina*, and *chachma sheb'bina* activates *bina sheb'chachma*. Then, seeing with the eye of *chachma*, one will understand not just the *seder hishtalshelus* but one thing from another: from the *seder hishtalshelus*, something deeper than the *seder hishtalshelus*. This is the power the book will discuss later. [▶ 5:12](listen:016#312)

## §33

**the source of the comprehension of a new insight within [the field of] his comprehension is also the matter of “understand with wisdom”**: the root of *chachma sheb'bina* is in *bina sheb'chachma*. **However, this remains concealed in the source of the intellect.** The one is in the hidden part of the intellect and the other in its revealed part. [▶ 5:55](listen:016#355)
