## §43

**it appears as if he is contracting and focusing the vessel of his brain**: that is the outward sign that someone is concentrating. He looks as if he is squeezing up his brain, his whole face squeezed together, so deeply is he connected to the concept. The text's Yiddish says he is wholly taken up and strongly squeezed together, contracted into one movement and one look: totally focused on one thing. [▶ 13:39](listen:017#819)

**It is specifically through this deep concentration that he can come to an essential and deep comprehension of the subject matter**. This book is always a *mashal* for itself. The whole book is really one continuous idea, and to do the *iyun* fully one finally has to hold it all in one's head; yet right now the class is squeezed into the subject of *daas* alone. It is not thinking about the *Malchus* of *tevuna*, or how the letters come out, or the difference between *chachma* and *bina*. Thinking about them now would actually take away from going deep into this. A good student asks according to the subject; one who keeps asking about other matters learns nothing. What brings one to the real depth of the concept is squeezing oneself into this one matter and leaving all the others aside. [▶ 15:16](listen:017#916)

## §44–45

**the very opposite of contraction and focus**. The *iyun* of *bina* means cutting a matter up into little pieces. **Although, initially, [the analysis of *Binah*] begins with focus and contraction, it nonetheless immediately spreads forth with great expansiveness**: it starts with one point, but at once spreads into all its parts (in this book: the *Malchus* of *tevuna*, the letters, the outer and the inner, the angels). That is what *hisbonenus* means, cutting a matter into all its sections and parts. [▶ 17:33](listen:017#1053)

*Daas* is just the opposite. **On the contrary, [the concentration] is only the contraction and focus**: not going into everything, but contracting away everything else, **gathering all of one’s mental capacities to connect only to this specific matter**. So the class takes the whole power of the mind and puts it on *daas* alone, although there are so many other things to think about. It is exactly the opposite of *bina*. [▶ 18:40](listen:017#1120)

## §46

**This is the very opposite of the spreading forth of the analysis with a great breadth into the subject matter.** In the text's Yiddish, he busies himself with this one thing and nothing else. **However, it is through the delving of the concentration that one can come to the depth of the concept, until its very source in the depths of *Chochmah* and *Binah*.** This does not mean staying with one point forever, going over it again and again in a cave in the Himalayas, which is basically what they do there. The other points will come: the next chapter is about *tevuna*, and then *daas* will be set aside and the whole concentration put on that. When doing a particular point, one is totally concentrated on that point, and that is what brings one to the actual depth of the concept, as it is in the depth of *chachma* and *bina*, in *taalumos chachma*. [▶ 19:25](listen:017#1165)

## §47

**reaching the aspect of the upper *Da’at* which unites *Chochmah* and *Binah***: *daas elyon* (higher knowledge), which, like *Keser*, is above *chachma* and *bina* and puts them together. It means one knows the thing: one learned it and now knows it. The lower *daas*, *daas tachton*, is only the vessel. *Daas elyon* means the thing is now part of one's mind, connected to oneself, one with oneself, as "Adam knew Chava": they became one. The whole of *chachma* and *bina* is now joined together in the *Keser*. [▶ 21:37](listen:017#1297)

*Daas* **is hidden in the mouth of** *bina*. The mouth is like *Malchus*, an upper *Malchus*: when one wants to bring *bina* out into speech, as in teaching, one has to bring *daas* into it. The class is not sitting down to concentrate like the yogis; it is trying to understand, and to give a lesson. In talking it out, the concentration comes by itself and *daas* is revealed, because that is where it was hidden in the first place. [▶ 22:42](listen:017#1362)

## §48

Moshe, who is *daas*, **merited *Binah***, the fiftieth gate. Moshe is *daas*, but a Jewish *daas*, one that goes specifically into *bina*. A *daas* unconnected to *bina*, where each of the *sefiros* stands on its own, is just concentration, on nothing in particular. It was because Moshe was connected to the matter that he merited *bina*. **These [two faculties] are interdependent**, as it says: if there is no *daas* there is no *bina*, and if there is no *bina* there is no *daas*. If you do not concentrate on your lesson you will understand nothing; and if there is nothing to understand you will lose interest. Without the lower *daas* of concentrating on the lesson there will be no understanding, and without understanding there will be no *daas elyon*, and you will not know anything. Concentration with no explanation does not rise from *daas tachton* to *daas elyon*: one just sits there and concentrates and knows nothing, and if one does seem to come to know something, it is a vain imagination. [▶ 24:13](listen:017#1453)
