## §27

**“Understand with wisdom and be wise in understanding.”** "Wise in understanding" is *chachma* of *bina*. **This is [the ability] to understand a new insight within the comprehension of *Binah***: a new insight, but in this same *bina*. [▶ 31:16](listen:015#1876)

> **Mashal:** An innovation in computers is not made by someone who meditated in the Himalayas but by people who work with computers over and over. They could not have thought about the innovation, which they did not know yet; they thought about what they already knew, and came up with something new. So too the engineers who make better cars learned in college about the cars we already have. And the invention is in their own field: a computer engineer will not invent a new drug, he will invent a better computer. That is *chachma sheb'bina*. [▶ 31:39](listen:015#1899)

It works through *bina sheb'bina* within the *kli* of *daas*. Someone who studies computers only to become a millionaire, with no real interest in computers, has *bina sheb'bina* without the *kli* of *daas*, and will probably invent nothing. Yet a vessel alone is also useless: a glass will not help a man dying of thirst in the desert, and water thrown at him without a glass spills into the sand. [▶ 34:28](listen:015#2068)

**This only comes through the aspect of *Iyun***. One cannot decide, "Now I'm going to innovate something." He cites a tract of the Rebbe Rashab on how to learn Talmud: one should not go after a *chiddush* (new insight). Trying to make one produces a "real" *chiddush*, something invented out of one's own head that was not told to *Moshe Rabbeinu* at Sinai. The true *chiddush* of a seasoned student comes when he learns the text first, and then with Rashi. Whoever wants to be an innovator will never be one; it is specifically through *iyun* that the *chiddush* comes by itself. [▶ 36:22](listen:015#2182)

This is what the whole chapter is about: *bina sheb'bina* within the vessel of *daas* activates all the powers, above it and below it. The arousal of the *middos* (emotional attributes) comes from *hisbonenus*, and so too the depth of *chachma*, which one really wants, is reached not by meditating on the light but through *iyun*. Even *daas*, one's connection to a matter, is activated by it: the more one understands about anything, the more one's *daas* toward it is activated. [▶ 37:33](listen:015#2253)

*Iyun* is the analysis **which clarifies all the particular parts until they are clear**, separating a thing into its parts by their classes. A personality, for example, divides into intellect and emotions; intellect into *chachma*, *bina* and *daas*, with *Keser* above them; the emotions into seven, basically love and fear; and each of those divides further. **This *Iyun* (analysis) is what is called *Binah* of *Binah*.** [▶ 39:39](listen:015#2379)

## §28

**After [a person has done this] he will be capable of bringing new insight into understanding and comprehension, but only within the subject matter itself.** Only after this analysis. It is specifically the people who know every part of a car who can come up with a better car; someone with no idea how the motor works cannot. And only within that field. The Wright brothers took a motor from a car and put it on an airplane, but that is still a machine for travel, and is really a matter of *tevuna* (drawing an idea out), going from one field to another. The basic meaning of *chachma sheb'bina* is innovation within this *hasaga* itself. [▶ 41:18](listen:015#2478)

**Now, *Keter* of *Binah* is the aforementioned depth of the comprehension**. The *Keser* of *bina* has an outer level, which is just understanding the explanation, but its root is in the depth of *chachma*: what one really wants to understand is not just the explanation but the actual thing. [▶ 42:43](listen:015#2563)
