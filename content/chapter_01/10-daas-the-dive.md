## §38

**is not the actual delving into the depth of the comprehension**: *ha'amakas hadaas* is the deepening, not the depth itself.

> **Mashal:** When you take a deep dive off a board, *ha'amakas hadaas* is the dive; the depth is the depth of the pool. Or the divers in Hawaii who jump off their little boats after money thrown into the water: the depth is the floor of the ocean, and the dive is what gets them there. [▶ 0:59](listen:017#59)

So **the faculty of *Da’at* has no relation to it**. The actual depth of a concept has nothing to do with concentration. To understand the depth of how computers work, one has to concentrate at school to get it, but the concept itself has nothing to do with concentration. [▶ 1:47](listen:017#107)

## §39–40

**the faculty of *Da’at* is the aspect of the strong connection of one’s feelings to a concept**: concentration, connecting one's feeling very much into the concept. This is what **brings one to the depth of the comprehended concept**, after the analysis. Knowing all the details is still not enough. Two children in a classroom hear exactly the same explanation from the same teacher, and one gets A's and the other D's: one had *daas* and the other did not, so one reached the depth of the concept and the other did not. Yet it is only **after the aforementioned analysis**. A child who decides that since he concentrates so hard he will stay home and concentrate will get nowhere, because he never got the explanation. *Daas* is only a vessel for going deep into the *hasaga*; one has to have the *hasaga*. [▶ 2:21](listen:017#141)

**Now, *Da’at* – concentration, also possesses the three dimensions of length, width and depth.** The length of *daas* is, first, the attention span. It is also the power to hold an idea whole: to do the proper contemplation with *daas* once one has the depth of the concept, one has to hold the entire book in one's head at once, or at least the matter at hand, such as this paragraph about *daas*. And how long can one concentrate? With an attention span of three seconds, learning together is impossible; these classes need one to set everything else aside for half an hour to an hour. In some works a single idea runs 30 to 100 chapters, and really the whole work is one idea; holding it takes an incredible length of *daas*. [▶ 3:41](listen:017#221)

**those who have a long attention span** can sit with one thing much longer; children, generally, have a short attention span. **There are those who have a broad attention span, but not a long attention span**: one who knows a little of everything but cannot stay with one thing for long. A strong *daas* means being strongly opinionated; a light *daas* means changing one's opinion easily. On the words **women who have a light** *daas*, he stresses that this is a comparison of people on the same level; it does not rule out that some women have a stronger *daas* than certain men. [▶ 9:08](listen:017#548) A *frum* woman who is into learning could rightly say that most men do not have as weighty a *daas* as she does. But compare a man who is into rock and roll with his girlfriend, and her *daas* will be lighter still than his. Likewise the wife of one of the *gedolim* may have more *daas* than you, but compared with her husband it may not be so: it is according to the level. [▶ 9:37](listen:017#577) It means **because they lack the depth of concentration, subsequently their concentration is short**, like a little child's: one who is not deeply into a thing has a short *daas* for it. As with *bina*, the depth brings about the width and the length. [▶ 8:30](listen:017#510)

> **Mashal:** He knew a boy who seemed to have no attention span for anything at school and did poorly in mathematics, but loved hockey. He had books on hockey with whole pages of statistics that looked like mathematics books, and he could sit with them for hours. According to the depth of one's *daas* in something, its length grows longer. [▶ 10:56](listen:017#656)

## §41–42

**The difference between a weak concentration and a deep concentration can be understood by the difference between a child and an adult**: a child **only possesses a very external connection or feelings towards the object of his understanding or desire**. A child wants a toy so much, gets it, and two seconds later has forgotten it. Why, when he wanted it so much? Because the connection was only external; it did not touch him at an inner level. [▶ 11:50](listen:017#710)

**In contrast, an adult has a deep connection to the object of his understanding and desire**, like a committed communist, bound to it in a far more inward way. That is called the depth of *daas*, and **this automatically draws out a length of concentration and a width of concentration**. Someone who is really deep into a thing automatically has a long attention span, can hold long ideas in his head, and has the width to see it in many different ways, just as with *bina* the length and width come from the depth. [▶ 12:52](listen:017#772)
