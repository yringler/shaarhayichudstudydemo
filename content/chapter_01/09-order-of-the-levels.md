## §34

**We find that the order of levels is as follows**: now the text tells how *hisbonenus* is done. One starts with *bina sheb'bina*, going through the analysis of all the separate parts, **to clarify and distinguish between all the inner and outer specifics, with the utmost precision**: to examine, as far as examining can go, the inner and outer details of whatever one is examining. [▶ 6:38](listen:016#398)

## §35

From *bina sheb'bina* also comes the length of *bina*: **to lower the concept through many investments**, explaining it down into *meshalim* (analogies) until a child can grasp it. So *bina sheb'bina* is also the length of *bina*. But it does not have **the aspect of a width, with various explanations of all its angles**. Different explanations are already an innovation, and that is not *bina sheb'bina*. *Bina sheb'bina* understands within this explanation only, along this one length. [▶ 7:08](listen:016#428)

## §36

The width is *chachma sheb'bina*: **to bring forth insights, [but only] in the specific subject**, a new twist on the matter, a new *inyan* (idea), but only in this *hasaga*. **This is [the aspect of] the width, from which the aspect of *Binah* of *Binah* creates a length**: once one has a new twist on a matter, one can make a whole new length by going and analyzing that twist. [▶ 7:46](listen:016#466)

## §37

**the extent of the length and width of *Chochmah* and *Binah* is dependent upon the aspect of the depth of the comprehension**. In *Chassidus* the length and width can be tremendous, because the depth is tremendous.

> **Mashal:** Everyone understands that chess is a deeper game than checkers, so all kinds of books of strategy are written on chess. On checkers one can also write, but how much? The depth of chess is greater, so its length and width are greater. [▶ 8:51](listen:016#531)

It also works the other way, which the text does not say here though it is really the main point of the chapter: one reaches the depth only by going through the length and the width, not just by concentrating on it. A doctor who studied for seven years went through the length; one who also knows other kinds of medicine has a width, and because of it may have a greater depth. [▶ 9:37](listen:016#577)

The text describes it from above to below, as a teacher presents it: from the depth comes the width, and from the width the length. The student goes from below to above, and should concentrate specifically on the length. If he tries for the width he will just jump around: a physicist one day, a chemist the next, then computers, then airplanes. That is a wide *daas* without a deep one. A width is many lengths, and to learn one does *bina sheb'bina*, a single length. Someone who has learned other Kabbalistic works may object, "It says *chachma* is not this!" That is a different length, to be set aside while learning this one. After going through the length of this book, one will understand how *chachma* both has letters and does not, is a point and yet has dimensions: two explanations, which together give a width. [▶ 12:20](listen:016#740)

So the order runs from *bina sheb'bina*, one length, to *chachma sheb'bina*, new things within this explanation; then to the outer *bina sheb'chachma*, the potential of many explanations; that activates *chachma sheb'chachma*; and from there the inner *bina sheb'chachma* reaches *taalumos chachma*, understanding one thing from another. Understanding that the river spoken of here is not a river, going from the *mashal* to the *nimshal* (what the analogy stands for), is itself this power. [▶ 15:15](listen:016#915)

It is said that Moshiach will open up the river into seven streams: along its length, not across it. Going up a river leads to the spring and on to the aquifer, and he will open it all the way, so that the hidden wellsprings of the deep burst out, as in the Flood. [▶ 17:00](listen:016#1020)

One might ask why the text uses Kabbalistic Names for *chachma* of *bina* and *bina* of *chachma*, when the explanation would be the same without them. The book was written in its time for Kabbalists, and later in the book the Names will come into play; for now it is enough to understand *chachma* of *bina* and *bina* of *chachma*. [▶ 19:12](listen:016#1152)
