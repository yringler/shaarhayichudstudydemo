## §28–29

**This is likewise so in the contemplation of the particulars of the world of *Atzilut*, in its lights and vessels**: for example **the light of *Chessed* of *Malchut* of *Atzilut*, or** of **the light of *Chessed* of *Chochmah* of *Atzilut***. He understands the relationships, and **how they were emanated**. [▶ 23:13](listen:060#1393)

**This can be comprehended by way of analogy from how light is emanated into revelation from its concealment in the essence**. [▶ 23:32](listen:060#1412) Again, it is not that he understands what *Atzilus* really is; he understands the relationships within *Atzilus* by the analogies down here. [▶ 23:44](listen:060#1424)

## §30

**This is also similar to how Moshe, “emanated from his spirit,”**: just as *Moshe* emanated of his spirit onto the elders, and *Moshe* lost nothing, **and the like**, the various other analogies. [▶ 23:54](listen:060#1434)

## §31

This too can be understood with real comprehension: **he nonetheless will comprehend how** the *middos* (attributes) of *Atzilus* **come into being**. [▶ 24:08](listen:060#1448) He grasps this even though **the emotive qualities (*Midot*) of the world of *Atzilut* are only in the aspect of sources, and are separate from the G-dly influence as it is within creation**. The *middos* of *Atzilus* themselves have nothing to do with *Beriya*, *Yetzira* and *Asiya*. Though they are their sources, they are entirely separate from them; the worlds of *Beriya*, *Yetzira* and *Asiya* come out only from *Malchus* of *Malchus* of *Atzilus*, not even from *Malchus* of *Atzilus*. And he can understand this: how the *middos* of *Atzilus* are separate sources. [▶ 24:34](listen:060#1474)

## §32

**However, the aspect of the true “nothingness” that shines upon them from the light of the Essential Self** of the Emanator **cannot be grasped even through intellectual comparisons**: it cannot be understood even through an image. [▶ 25:02](listen:060#1502) **Rather, it may only be [grasped] in a concealed fashion**, as said before: it is forced on the mind, one must say that it must be so. [▶ 25:13](listen:060#1513)

This **is the aspect of the intellectual sight, which is higher than actual comprehension and grasp**: the intellect sees that there is something higher than the understanding itself. [▶ 25:24](listen:060#1524) At every level of understanding in *Chassidus* there are two things: one understands the actual explanation of how the "something" comes from the "nothing", and at the same time one sees that there is something entirely beyond the understanding. And they have to come together. [▶ 25:35](listen:060#1535)

> **Key point:** The introduction to *Etz Chaim* and many kabbalistic and Chassidic books say that if you think you actually understand, you do not understand. "But here, in this book, I understand!" Yes, you understand the explanation and how something comes from nothing. That does not mean you should not understand; just the opposite: you should understand that there is something beyond understanding. If you do not get that, you have lost the whole point. Even what you understand you have not understood properly, because it did not point you to what is beyond understanding. If you did not see how fantastically removed *Malchus* of *Malchus* of *Asiya* is from you, you did not really understand how the "something" came out of the "nothing". [▶ 26:06](listen:060#1566)

## §33–34

**This is like the saying, “No thought can grasp You,” which refers to the thought of *Binah*.** [▶ 0:10](listen:061#10) The thought of understanding, of the explanations, does not grasp the essence of the thing at all. [▶ 0:20](listen:061#20)

**In other words, this is the opposite of comprehension**: the sight of *chachma* is the exact opposite of *hasaga*. [▶ 0:28](listen:061#28) For **the more he delves [into it], the more astounding will the essential light of the Emanator be for him**: the more he learns and the more deeply he thinks, the more amazingly far it is from him. [▶ 0:41](listen:061#41)

## §35

**The indication of this** is that **a very great aspect of nullification is born [in him]**: a tremendous humility. [▶ 1:02](listen:061#62) Not humility in comparison to the ten-foot man, but what he feels when he realizes what a real giant is, like *Og* king of Bashan: "There is no way I can go up against him." Even *Moshe Rabbeinu* went up against him only because the *Aibershter* promised to help him. When *Moshe* saw *Og*, he was worried: this one picks up mountains and throws them at us; how will we fight him? It is a matter of total *bittul* and humility. [▶ 1:19](listen:061#79)

This is where real humility comes from. Most people think humility means being humble because you are five feet tall and the other man is ten. Here it is entirely beyond comparison: "I am nothing, not even an inch tall. The whole universe, with its suns, is nothing compared to *Malchus* of *Asiya*, never mind the light of the Emanator. What do I have to talk about?" [▶ 2:02](listen:061#122)

What people think of as humility is really lowliness (*shiflus*), and one is not supposed to be lowly, thinking "I am not such a big shot, I don't understand so well." Just the opposite: he is a big shot. He has the comprehension of *bina*, he knows he has it, he is happy with it, and he knows it is good for him. But compared to the actual wonder, it makes no difference how big he is: he is still nothing. [▶ 2:43](listen:061#163)
