## §36–37

It follows that **the [opposite] aspects of nullification and joy, which branch out and are born of the *Chochmah* and *Binah* [that is, the contemplation] of each particular, specifically come together, as one**: they come simultaneously. [▶ 3:31](listen:061#211) If they do not come together, one is not studying *Chassidus* properly. [▶ 3:44](listen:061#224)

This is what is seen with many kabbalists: they have one or the other. Either they have the understanding and become real big shots, thinking that now they are really something; or they do not want to understand anything, and want only some sense of wonder and humility. But that is not real humility, since without comprehension there is no real wonder either: it is false humility. [▶ 3:51](listen:061#231)

**This is true of all *Hitbonenut-*contemplation of each particular detail, specifically**: every contemplation in this whole book has both. [▶ 4:27](listen:061#267)

## §38

**These two aspects are interdependent**: if one is missing, the other is missing too. [▶ 4:36](listen:061#276) **If he has no joy from the thorough comprehension of the “something,” then he will also not come to the astonishment and sense of distance in his inability to grasp the “nothingness.”** If he has no understanding of the "something", he has no real wonder at the "nothing". [▶ 4:44](listen:061#284)

One says: "I just have the wonder. I believe in G-d, I am humble, a simple little fellow; I don't need all this understanding." The answer: How can you be a simple little fellow? You have no understanding, so you have no wonder, and you are not really humble: you are really proud (*baal gaava*). "What do you mean? I don't think I'm anything." Yes, you do. Even if it is a tiny bit, you think you are something, because you lack the comprehension of *bina*. [▶ 5:00](listen:061#300)

## §39

**The degree of his joy is, literally, commensurate to the degree of his nullification**: as happy as he is, and as great as he realizes he is becoming, truly becoming a master of the deepest secrets, so great is his *bittul*, so humble does he become. [▶ 5:38](listen:061#338)

For **his comprehension of the created is commensurate to his astonishment at the separateness of the Creator**: exactly as far as he grasps how the "something" comes into being, so far is his wonder at how fantastically far above us the *Aibershter* is. [▶ 5:57](listen:061#357)

## §40

**This is an important principle**. It is not something said in passing; it is a great general principle (*klal gadol*), like "Love your fellow as yourself is a great principle of the Torah." [▶ 6:15](listen:061#375) It is the central principle of all of *Chassidus*: it applies to **every *Hitbonenut*-contemplation, whether general or particular**. [▶ 6:31](listen:061#391)
