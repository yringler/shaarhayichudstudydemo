## §19

**Accordingly, all those who contemplate in a manner of particulars**, anyone who learns properly in the particular way that chapter 4 explained, **will become [aware of] these two aspects through their contemplation; the concealed aspect and the revealed aspect.** [▶ 14:17](listen:060#857)

## §20

The concealed is the "nothing" that creates; the revealed is the "something" that is created. [▶ 14:32](listen:060#872) Accordingly, **the arousal that is born of it [is comprised of] two opposites**, at the same time. [▶ 14:46](listen:060#886) **“His heart is worried within him”** **because he comprehends the astounding greatness of the G-dly “nothing” that brings [everything] into being**, and this gives him a feeling of nothingness, as if he were nothing at all. [▶ 14:57](listen:060#897)

This is where true humility comes from, not from lecturing oneself into it. True humility is not "I am not as great as Einstein": that is like saying "I am not as tall as Magic Johnson." There is still some comparison. [▶ 15:12](listen:060#912)

> **Mashal:** A man who has seen *Og* king of Bashan is taken to the circus to see a giant, and is shown a man ten feet tall. "That's a giant?" he says. He is used to a giant who can lift a mountain. *Moshe Rabbeinu* was eighteen feet tall, jumped eighteen feet in the air with an eighteen-foot sword, fifty-four feet in all, and struck *Og* in the ankle. So the more one understands, the more *batel* one is: the greater the wonder, the bigger the giant. Before, he thought a ten-foot man was a giant; now he sees that jumping fifty feet up would only reach the ankle. [▶ 15:42](listen:060#942)

So there are the two: he truly worries at how far the thing is from him, and at the same time **he is [simultaneously] joyful because of his thorough grasp of how all that exists comes into being**, how the "something" comes out of the "nothing". [▶ 17:03](listen:060#1023)
