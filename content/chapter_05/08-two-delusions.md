## §41

**In contrast, if it appears to someone that he thoroughly grasps the aspect of creation, but he lacks the aspect of nullification to the Creator, then, without a doubt, it is a certainty that he has not grasped anything.** If no *bittul* is seen in him, he understood nothing, and the text says so: certainly, without a doubt. [▶ 6:42](listen:061#402)

## §42–43

Likewise, **if someone imagines that he possesses the aspect of nullification through the realization of the wondrousness of the Creator**, thinking, "I have *bittul* because I think about G-d's greatness," **but does not thoroughly comprehend the manner of how [everything] comes into being**, and does not know the matters of *seder hishtalshelus*, **it is a false imagination and he has no nullification whatsoever**. He is not humble at all. [▶ 7:01](listen:061#421)

**These two are interdependent and commensurate to each other**: one depends on the other, in exact proportion. As much as one understands in *Chassidus*, so humble is one; and as *batel* as one is, so much does one understand in *Chassidus*. [▶ 7:32](listen:061#452)

The next class (part 3) returns to this. If a person feels he is nothing next to G-d but something next to someone else, he is not truly humble. Before the *Aibershter* he is nothing, really nothing; and whoever knows the greatness of the *Aibershter* knows that he is really nothing. [▶ 1:31](listen:062#91) As the chapter says, without the great sense of self that comes from *bina*, there is no humility: the one who grasps feels it is good, is happy with it, and knows it is making him bigger, and "commensurate to the degree of his joy is the degree of his *bittul*." [▶ 5:35](listen:062#335)

These are the two basic sicknesses: *bina* without *chachma*, or *chachma* without *bina*. [▶ 7:11](listen:062#431) There are those who say "we are the ones who serve (*ovdim*); you are just intellectuals (*maskilim*); the real thing is *avoda*, and you are into comprehension." But without comprehension there is no *bittul*, so their *bittul* and their *avoda* are nothing; as the chapters so far explain, without contemplation there is no *avoda*. [▶ 7:31](listen:062#451) Someone says, "I am *batel* to the *Rebbe*; why do I need to study this?" Then he is neither *batel* to the *Rebbe* nor to the *Aibershter*; it is all false imaginings. The *Rebbe* is *Chassidus*: not wanting to know the particulars of *Chassidus* means not really wanting to know about the *Rebbe*. [▶ 8:11](listen:062#491)

The verse says: "Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom… but let him who glories glory in this: understanding and knowing Me, that I am G-d who does kindness, justice and righteousness in the earth, for in these I delight." It does not say this is permitted; it says one should glory in it, like a *mitzva*. [▶ 8:44](listen:062#524) To know Him as G-d all the way up, who does justice and righteousness all the way down, and "in these I delight": these things down here are what He wanted up there. The will in the Essence extends all the way down. This describes the whole *seder hishtalshelus*: to know it, not mechanically, but knowing that it is truly the *Aibershter* and what He wanted. [▶ 10:20](listen:062#620)

On the other side are those with *bina* and no *bittul*. Their lack of *bittul* shows that they do not understand what they study, so they have no *bina* either. They are the "big shots" of *Chassidus* who know a great many separate points but cannot put them together. [▶ 12:16](listen:062#736)

## §44–45

**This is known to all who have tasted true *Hitbonenut*, and do not at all delude themselves.** [▶ 7:59](listen:061#479) It **is likewise understood by those who are accustomed to the teachings of *Chassidut* from their youth. However, a great many people delude themselves in all this, in many different ways**: they think they know *Chassidus*, and they think they are humble. [▶ 8:09](listen:061#489)

But humility is not lowliness. *Moshe Rabbeinu* was the humblest man there was, yet in various places he acted like a king, and when they did not listen to him he became angry. What kind of humility is that? He was comparing himself with the *Aibershter*: not that he did not know his own level, just the opposite. [▶ 8:33](listen:061#513) What the text says here is the opposite of the common view: if you are not a big shot in *Chassidus*, you are not a simple person. You cannot call yourself a simpleton who "just believes in G-d" when you know no *Chassidus*, because the two depend on each other. [▶ 9:06](listen:061#546)

This also connects with later matters: it is the joy of *bina* and the *bittul* of *chachma*, which belongs with the chapters on the union of *Abba*, a constant union: "two companions who never part". There cannot be one without the other: without *bina* there is no *chachma*, and without *chachma* no *bina*. One cannot have only the understanding of *bina*, or only the *bittul* of *chachma*, as some say: "just blank out your mind." That is *chachma*? There is no *chachma* without *bina*. [▶ 9:33](listen:061#573)
