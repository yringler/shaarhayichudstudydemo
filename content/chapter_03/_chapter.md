## Overview

Chapter three is a conclusion of the first two chapters, applying their terms to contemplating G-dliness, with the example of *memalei kol almin*, the coming into being of something from nothing. *Bina* is the essential grasp of the concept in all its particulars. Its length brings the concept close to the mind through physical analogies, until even a child can grasp it. Its width explains the same concept in many ways, through several lines of analogy. Only after length and width does one reach the depth, which can look like a brief summary but is not. *Daas* connects a person to what he has understood and leads to the sight of the intellect. *Tevuna* brings the hidden depth down into what one actually sees, until the world itself is seen as G-dliness. Without it, Chassidus stays an abstract idea, and a person asks what it is good for.
